Item specifics are the structured fields on an eBay listing: brand, size,
colour, department, type, material and so on. They are separate from the
title and the description, and they are the only part of a listing that
eBay&rsquo;s search filters can read. When eBay says a listing is missing
required item specifics, it means one of those fields is empty or holds a
value eBay does not recognise, and it will not let the listing go live until
you fill it in.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">
A listing is refused when a required specific is empty or has a made-up
value. For clothing in the UK that is almost always Brand, Size, Colour,
Department or Type. The sell form marks every required field, so fill those
first, pick eBay&rsquo;s suggested value rather than typing your own, and
check Seller Hub&rsquo;s missing specifics filter for the listings you
already have up.
</Callout>

## What item specifics are

eBay&rsquo;s [Seller Centre](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/listings/item-specifics)
describes them as fields that &ldquo;may include brand, size, type, colour,
style or other relevant info about the item you&rsquo;re selling&rdquo;. Each
one is a name and a value: Brand is the name, Nike is the value. The names
depend on the category you list in. A pair of trainers gets UK Shoe Size and
Upper Material; a dress gets Dress Length and Sleeve Length.

They matter because eBay does not read your description the way a buyer
does. Its filters, its category pages and a lot of its search ranking work
from these fields. Write &ldquo;size 12 black Zara dress&rdquo; in the title
and leave the Size field empty, and a buyer who ticks Size 12 in the filter
panel will never see it.

## Required, recommended and additional

eBay sorts the specifics for every category into three groups, and the
difference decides whether your listing goes live.

- **Required.** The Seller Centre says &ldquo;New or revised listings that
  don&rsquo;t include required item specifics will be blocked.&rdquo; These
  are the ones with a mark next to them in the sell form. Brand, Size and
  Colour are the examples eBay itself gives as &ldquo;critical to the buyer
  experience&rdquo;.
- **Recommended.** Not needed to list, but eBay says they &ldquo;improve
  your potential buyers&rsquo; search experience&rdquo;. They feed the
  filters and the category pages, so a listing without them is live but
  easy to miss.
- **Additional.** Everything else eBay offers for the category. Useful when
  a buyer is picky, otherwise optional.

There is a fourth label you will see from time to time: **Required soon**.
eBay adds new required specifics a few times a year and flags them in the
sell form with the date they become mandatory. Its
[April 2022 notice](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2022-april/item-specifics)
is a typical example: new fields appeared in May and became required in
July. A listing that was fine when you wrote it can need a new field the
next time you revise it.

## Why eBay refuses a listing

eBay blocks a listing at the point you save it, and the message is nearly
always one of these four.

1. **A required specific is empty.** The most common one by far. The sell
   form highlights the field and will not let you continue.
2. **The value is not one eBay recognises.** You can type a custom value in
   many fields, but for some, Size being the big one, eBay now only accepts
   its own list. Its
   [listing help](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/creating-listing?id=4105)
   says that from August 2026 apparel and footwear listings with
   &ldquo;unclear or missing size or condition details may be hidden until
   updated&rdquo;, and names &ldquo;See description&rdquo; and
   &ldquo;N/A&rdquo; as the kind of value it will flag.
3. **The category is wrong.** Each category has its own set of specifics.
   List a women&rsquo;s coat under Men&rsquo;s Coats and eBay asks for a
   different Size list and a different Department, and your values stop
   matching.
4. **A placeholder in a required identifier.** eBay&rsquo;s
   [item description policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/listing-policies/item-description-policy?id=4372)
   says that &ldquo;Does not apply&rdquo;, unbranded or generic, and
   &ldquo;N/A&rdquo; are not allowed in required product identifiers such
   as EAN or MPN. Second-hand clothes rarely need those, but a listing can
   still be refused for it if you pick a category that does.

The same policy says the consequences of ignoring it run from
&ldquo;removing the listing&rdquo; through warnings to &ldquo;restricting
activity or account suspension&rdquo;. In practice an empty field just
stops the listing. A wrong or misleading value on a live listing is what
leads to removal.

Two of these catch sellers out again and again. **Department** is not the
same as the category: eBay wants Men, Women, Unisex Adult, Boys or Girls as
a field, even if the category already says Women&rsquo;s. **Size Type**
(Regular, Petite, Tall, Plus, Maternity) sits next to Size on many clothing
categories and is easy to scroll past.

## How to see the required list before you list

You do not have to guess.

- **In the sell form.** Pick the category first, then scroll to Item
  specifics. The required fields sit at the top with a mark, and the
  dropdown values under each one are eBay&rsquo;s own list. The Seller
  Centre advises you to &ldquo;use eBay&rsquo;s suggested values as much as
  possible rather than creating your own custom values&rdquo;, because a
  custom value does not match a filter.
- **In Seller Hub, for listings already up.** The Active listings page has
  quick filters for listings missing Required, Required soon and
  Recommended specifics, and the Overview tab shows a task reminder when any
  listing is missing a required one. The Download button on the same page
  offers an Excel file of your listings with their specifics, which you can
  fill in and upload back.
- **In the app.** My eBay and the eBay app show which of your active
  listings have missing item specifics, according to the Seller Centre.

## What to put when you genuinely do not know

Most refusals come from fields that are hard to fill honestly on second-hand
stock: no label, a faded size, a brand you cannot read. Here is what works
and what does not.

- **Brand.** If there is no brand, eBay&rsquo;s own list includes
  Unbranded and Handmade. Pick the one that is true. Do not type the shop
  you bought it from, and do not put a designer name on an item that only
  looks like one.
- **Size.** Use the closest supported size. The item description policy
  says &ldquo;If an exact size is not available, select the closest
  supported size option&rdquo;, and that sellers &ldquo;must select the size
  that most accurately matches the manufacturer&rsquo;s size
  designation&rdquo;. Put the measurements in the description, not the Size
  field. &ldquo;See description&rdquo; in the Size field is exactly what
  eBay has said it will hide from August 2026.
- **Colour.** Pick the dominant colour from the list. Multicoloured is a
  valid value for prints.
- **Material.** Read the care label. If there is none, choose the closest
  from the list, or leave a recommended field empty rather than guess. Only
  a required field blocks the listing.
- **Product identifiers.** EAN, UPC and MPN are rarely required for clothes.
  When they are, the policy bans &ldquo;Does not apply&rdquo; and
  &ldquo;N/A&rdquo;. If the item really has no code, check whether you are
  in the right category; a used-clothing category normally does not ask.

A specific must be true. The policy also bans &ldquo;Including terms in the
wrong field&rdquo; and &ldquo;Including information that is not specific to
that field&rdquo;, so a Colour of &ldquo;Black Nike Hoodie M&rdquo; is a
policy breach, not a keyword trick.

## How specifics drive search and filters

Buyers on eBay rarely scroll past the first page. They search, then narrow
with the panel on the left or the filter sheet on mobile: Size, Brand,
Colour, Condition, Price. Every one of those filters reads a specific, not
the title. The Seller Centre puts it plainly: filling them in &ldquo;will
help to increase your product&rsquo;s visibility, especially when buyers use
the left-hand navigation filters&rdquo;, and some specifics &ldquo;can even
make your listings more likely to appear in Google Shopping results&rdquo;.

## Fixing the listings you already have

If you have been selling for a while, some of your live listings are
missing fields that have since become required. They stay live, because the
block only applies to new and revised listings, but every one of them is
invisible to the filters, and the next time you edit one eBay will stop you
until you fill it in.

**In Seller Hub.** Go to Listings, then Active, and use the
&ldquo;Item specifics: required&rdquo; quick filter. Select the listings and
click Edit to add specifics to several at once, or use Download, pick the
missing specifics file, fill in the spreadsheet and upload it. The
spreadsheet route is the quicker one above about 50 listings.

**In SaleLinx.** The [My Listings](/docs/inventory/listings) grid shows
your eBay listings next to your Depop and Vinted stock. Select the eBay
ones and click Edit: each card has the category, condition and item
specifics, and Save Changes sends them straight to eBay. The bulk panel in
that editor changes price, title, description and quantity across a
selection; specifics are set on each card. The
[eBay bulk editor](/features/ebay-bulk-edit) page has the overview.

Whichever you use, fix Size and Condition first on clothing and footwear.
Those are the two eBay has said it may hide listings for.

## Crosslisting from Vinted or Depop

Vinted and Depop have far fewer structured fields than eBay. Vinted has a
brand, a size, a condition and up to two colours. Depop has a brand, a size,
a condition and a colour. Neither has Department, Type, Style, Size Type or
Material. So when you copy a listing across, the eBay form has gaps that the
source listing cannot fill, and eBay refuses the copy.

Any crosslisting app has to deal with this in one of three ways: post what
it has and let eBay reject it, ask you for every missing field before it
posts, or work the missing ones out from the title and category and ask
only for what is left. SaleLinx&rsquo;s
[Vinted to eBay crosslister](/features/vinted-to-ebay) takes the third
route: it picks the eBay category, fills in brand, colour, size, department
and the rest from the Vinted listing, checks each item with eBay before
anything is posted, and shows a Fill in button on any row eBay would still
refuse. Copies to eBay go live, so that check is the review step. Our
[comparison of crosslisting apps](/blog/best-crosslisting-apps-uk) covers
how the others handle it.

If you crosslist by hand, do the eBay listing from the sell form with the
Vinted tab open beside it, and fill the required block before you touch the
description. The description is the part you can copy and paste; the
specifics are the part you cannot.

## Questions

### What does &ldquo;missing required item specifics&rdquo; mean on eBay?

One or more fields that eBay requires for that category is empty, or holds
a value eBay does not accept. The listing will not be saved until you fill
it in. In the sell form the field is highlighted; in Seller Hub the listing
appears under the Required quick filter.

### Which item specifics are required for clothing on eBay UK?

It varies by category, but Brand, Size, Colour, Department and Type are the
usual set, with Style, Material and a size measurement added in some
categories. Pick the category in the sell form and look at the marked
fields; that list is the current one.

### Can I put &ldquo;Does not apply&rdquo; in an item specific?

Not in a required product identifier. eBay&rsquo;s item description policy
bans &ldquo;Does not apply&rdquo;, &ldquo;N/A&rdquo; and unbranded or
generic in those fields. For Brand, pick Unbranded from eBay&rsquo;s list if
the item has no brand. For Size, pick the closest supported size and put
the measurements in the description.

### Do item specifics affect how many people see my listing?

Yes. eBay&rsquo;s search filters, its category pages and part of its ranking
read the specifics rather than the title. A listing with empty specifics is
live but drops out of every filtered search, and eBay says complete
specifics can also help listings appear in Google Shopping.

### Will my old listings be removed if they are missing specifics?

Not for an empty field. The block applies to new and revised listings, so
old ones stay live until you edit them. The exception is clothing and
footwear with an unclear or missing size or condition, which eBay has said
it may hide from August 2026 until updated. Fix those first.

### How do I add item specifics to lots of listings at once?

In Seller Hub, filter Active listings by missing required specifics, select
them and use Edit, or download the missing specifics spreadsheet, fill it in
and upload it. SaleLinx&rsquo;s My Listings editor opens each eBay listing
as a card with its specifics, so you can work through a filtered set without
leaving the panel. Our [eBay fees guide](/blog/ebay-fees-uk) covers what
each of those listings costs you once it sells.
