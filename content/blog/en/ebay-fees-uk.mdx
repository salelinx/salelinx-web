Selling on eBay UK has been free for private sellers since 2024, so the usual
answer to &ldquo;what are eBay&rsquo;s fees?&rdquo; is &ldquo;nothing&rdquo;.
That is only half of it. Buyers now pay a fee on private sellers&rsquo; items,
business sellers pay a percentage of every sale, and which kind of seller you
are is not entirely up to you. This guide sets out what each side pays, with a
link to eBay&rsquo;s own page for every figure, and how it compares with Depop
and Vinted. Everything was checked against those pages on 20 October 2026.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">
Private sellers in the UK pay no selling fee on eBay (except on vehicles) and
get 300 free listings a month. The buyer pays a Buyer Protection fee instead:
10p plus 7% of the first £20, so £1.50 on a £20 item. Business sellers&rsquo;
buyers pay no fee, but the seller pays eBay 11.9% on clothing plus 0.35%, both
worked out on the total including postage, and 30p or 40p per order, all
before VAT. If you buy things to resell, eBay says you must register as a
business, and it sets no minimum number of sales.
</Callout>

## Private or business: which are you?

eBay looks mainly at why you sell, not how much. Its
[business seller policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/selling-practices-policy/business-seller-policy?id=4710)
says a seller &ldquo;must register as a business if, for example, they sell
items they have bought to resell, they make items in order to sell them, or if
they buy items for their business&rdquo;. Its
[upgrade page](https://pages.ebay.co.uk/upgrade) adds that selling &ldquo;large
volumes each month&rdquo; also counts as business activity, but neither page
gives a number of sales below which reselling counts as private.

HMRC draws the line in the same place. Its guidance on
[income from online platforms](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms)
says you are probably trading if you sell goods you &ldquo;bought intending to
sell for a profit&rdquo;, and its worked example is someone buying from car
boot sales and charity shops to sell online for more than they paid.

- **Clearing out your own wardrobe or loft?** You are a private seller.
- **Buying stock to resell**, from charity shops, car boots, wholesalers or
  other sellers? eBay expects you to use a business account.

Three things to know before you switch:

- **You do not need a company.** eBay says you can have a business account
  as someone who is self-employed
  ([eBay](https://pages.ebay.co.uk/upgrade)).
- **It is one way.** &ldquo;Once you&rsquo;ve upgraded to a business account,
  it&rsquo;s not possible to switch back to a private account.&rdquo;
- **Posing as a private seller is not allowed.** UK consumer law treats
  &ldquo;falsely representing oneself as a consumer&rdquo; as unfair in all
  circumstances. The ban dates from 2008 and, since 6 April 2025, sits in the
  [Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Schedule 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20).
  eBay&rsquo;s policy also says business sellers &ldquo;can&rsquo;t represent
  themselves as private individuals&rdquo;.

## What private sellers pay

[eBay&rsquo;s fees page for private sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4364)
says: &ldquo;It&rsquo;s now free for UK-based private sellers to sell on eBay
(excluding motors: Cars, Motorcycles &amp; Vehicles listings).&rdquo; There is
no final value fee, no regulatory operating fee and no per-order fee. eBay made
pre-owned clothing free in April 2024
([eBay](https://www.ebayinc.com/stories/press-room/uk/ebay-uk-goes-free-to-sell-pre-owned-fashion-as-it-encourages-consumers-to-unlock-16.3-billion-of-unworn-clothes-sat-in-uk-wardrobes/))
and every other category except motors on 1 October 2024
([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sell)).

Listing is free too, up to a point. Private sellers get 300 free listings a
month and pay 35p for each one after that, and listing fees are not refunded.
If you have a few hundred one-off pieces and relist them by hand, 300 is the
limit you will hit first.

The rest is optional:

- **Promoted Listings.** A general campaign charges the ad rate you choose,
  from 2% to 100% of the total sale including postage, and only if the item
  sells within 30 days of someone clicking the ad
  ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/ebay-advertising/promoted-listings/general-campaign-strategy?id=4164)).
  A priority campaign charges per click, whether or not the item sells
  ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/promoted-listings-overview?id=5295)).
- **Listing upgrades.** A subtitle costs £2. Gallery Plus costs £2.50 but is
  free in Clothes, Shoes &amp; Accessories. Listing in a second category
  costs 35p.
- **A reserve price** on an auction costs 4% of the reserve (the lowest
  reserve is £50 and the fee is capped at £150), whether or not the item
  sells.
- **Selling abroad.** When the buyer&rsquo;s address is outside the UK, eBay
  charges a 3% international fee on the total, postage included.

These figures include VAT, except Promoted Listings, where eBay may add VAT to
the ad fee.

## What buyers pay: the Buyer Protection fee

The cost of free selling moved to the buyer. Anyone buying from a UK private
seller pays eBay a
[Buyer Protection fee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/buying/paying-items/buyer-protection-fee?id=5594)
made up of:

- a flat 10p per item;
- 7% of the item price up to £20;
- 4% of any part of the price from £20 to £300;
- 2% of any part of the price from £300 to £4,000.

Nothing more is added above £4,000, so the fee tops out at £86.70. It includes
VAT and is worked out on the item price (eBay&rsquo;s own examples add postage
on top). It is already included in the price buyers see when browsing, and
checkout shows it as a separate line. A buyer who gets a full refund gets the
fee back too. Buyers do not pay it on items from business sellers, or on cars,
motorcycles and vehicles.

([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/selling/benefits)). eBay cut the

### Offers include the fee

This one catches sellers out. On a private seller&rsquo;s listing, a
buyer&rsquo;s bid or offer includes the Buyer Protection fee
([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/selling/pricing-items/buyer-protection-information-sellers?id=5595)).
eBay&rsquo;s example is a £250 offer, which contains a £10.29 fee and leaves
the seller £239.71. eBay shows you the offer without the fee, so the number in
front of you is what you keep.

The catch is that the buyer&rsquo;s number and yours differ. A buyer who
offers £20 is offering you £18.60. When you weigh an eBay offer against one on
Depop or Vinted, compare what you would keep, not the buyer&rsquo;s headline
number.

## What business sellers pay

Business sellers&rsquo; buyers pay no Buyer Protection fee. The seller pays
eBay instead, and
[eBay&rsquo;s fees page for business sellers](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/business-selling-fees?id=4809)
sets out three charges on every sale:

- **A final value fee.** A percentage, set by category, of the total sale,
  &ldquo;which includes the item price, postage, and any applicable
  taxes&rdquo;.
- **A per-order fee.** 30p on orders of £10 or less and 40p on orders over
  £10. The 40p rate started on 12 February 2026
  ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change)).
- **A regulatory operating fee** of 0.35% of the total.

eBay quotes all of these before VAT.

Here is how that works on a £20 jacket with £3.50 postage, where the buyer
pays £23.50:

- final value fee, 11.9% of £23.50: £2.80;
- regulatory operating fee, 0.35% of £23.50: £0.08;
- per-order fee, as the order is over £10: £0.40.

That is £3.28 before VAT, or about 14% of what the buyer paid. eBay adds UK
VAT at 20% to business sellers&rsquo; fees, which makes it £3.93
([eBay Community](https://community.ebay.co.uk/t5/Announcements/Changes-to-VAT-on-eBay-fees-for-business-sellers-with-a-UK/ba-p/6241551)).
If you are registered for VAT you can usually reclaim it. If you are not, or
you use the flat rate scheme, it is a real cost.

A few more things change the bill:

- **eBay Shops** cost £27 (Basic), £77 (Featured) or £437 (Anchor) a month
  before VAT
  ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/listings/store-subscriptions?id=4122)).
  They give you more free listings and cheaper extra ones, not a lower final
  value fee, and you do not need one to sell as a business.
- **Below Standard sellers** pay an extra 6 percentage points of final value
  fee, or 7 after four months in a row. A clothing sale then costs 17.9%
  before the other two fees.
- **Top Rated sellers** get 10% off the percentage part of the final value
  fee on Premium Service listings.

### The gap is smaller than it looks

A private seller&rsquo;s £20 item shows to buyers as £21.50, because the
Buyer Protection fee is already in the price they see. A business seller can
list the same item at £21.50, which looks the same to a buyer browsing and
costs them the same at checkout, and keep £18.47 before VAT on fees. So at the
same price to the buyer, selling as a business costs you about £1.50 on a £20
item, not £2.85.

On fees alone, a private eBay account and Vinted both leave you the full
price, Depop&rsquo;s payment processing fee takes 88p of a £20 sale, and a
business eBay account costs the most. Buyers pay a similar extra everywhere,
roughly £1 to £2.50 on a £20 item, but only eBay publishes an exact formula.
If you list the same item on Depop and want to keep the same amount, price it
about 3% plus 30p higher.

How fast the money arrives differs too:

- **eBay.** For private sellers, money for tracked items becomes available 2
  days after delivery. Since 6 August 2025, private sellers with at least 10
  completed sales worth £150 or more in the last five years, and no more than
  2 cases or defects in the last 12 months, get it within 24 hours of the
  buyer paying
  ([eBay](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/getting-paid/getting-paid-items-youve-sold?id=4814)).
  Private sellers withdraw it whenever they like. Business sellers&rsquo;
  money is usually available about a day after the buyer pays, without
  waiting for delivery, and is paid out on a schedule, daily by default.
- **Depop.** With Depop Shipping, 2 business days after the item shows as
  delivered, or 10 business days after the sale if that comes first, then up
  to 5 days to reach your bank
  ([Depop](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791147-How-do-I-get-paid-UK)).
- **Vinted.** The order completes when the buyer confirms everything is fine,
  or 2 days after delivery, and the money reaches your Vinted balance within
  2 days of that ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/235)). A withdrawal
  to your bank then takes up to 5 business days
  ([Vinted](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/68)).

eBay completed its purchase of Depop on 30 July 2026, but Depop runs as its
own platform with its own fees
([Depop](https://news.depop.com/ebay-completes-acquisition-of-depop/)).

## Postage on eBay: Simple Delivery

For private sellers,
[Simple Delivery](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/selling/posting-items/setting-postage-options/simple-delivery?id=5575)
&ldquo;is the only delivery method available for private sellers who list
eligible items on eBay.co.uk&rdquo;. That covers most items up to £750, apart
from smartphones over £300 and some restricted items. You choose whether the
buyer or you pays for postage. When the item sells, you get a prepaid label or
QR code for Evri, Royal Mail, InPost or DHL, valid for 7 days.

eBay charges nothing extra for using Simple Delivery and adds no fee on top of
the label price, which you or the buyer pay. But if the parcel turns out
bigger or heavier than the size you chose, you pay the difference plus a £1
admin fee, so weigh and measure before you list. In return, eBay deals with
delivery problems with the buyer. Business sellers keep their own delivery
options ([eBay](https://pages.ebay.co.uk/upgrade)).

## Selling the same item on Depop or Vinted

eBay allows it, with a condition. Its
[selling practices policy](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/selling-practices-policy?id=4346)
says you must not list an item &ldquo;that you&rsquo;re simultaneously selling
outside of eBay without ensuring beforehand that you have enough product
available&rdquo;. With one-off second-hand pieces, that means ending the eBay
listing as soon as the item sells somewhere else.

If an eBay buyer buys the item before you end the listing and you have to
cancel, it counts against you. eBay&rsquo;s
[seller standards](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/selling-policies/seller-performance-policy?id=4347)
treat cancelling an order &ldquo;because they sold it to someone else&rdquo;
as a transaction defect. You can have defects on up to 2% of your
transactions, and you are only rated Below Standard once your defects involve
more than 4 different buyers. A Below Standard rating can push your listings
down in search, lower your selling limits and hold your funds, and for
business sellers it brings the 6-point fee surcharge above.

## Returns and the Money Back Guarantee

Almost every sale paid through eBay&rsquo;s checkout, private or business, is
covered by the
[eBay Money Back Guarantee](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210).
Vehicles and a few other categories are not.

- A buyer can report an item that never arrived up to 30 days after the
  estimated delivery date, or return one that is not as described within 30
  days of delivery.
- Saying &ldquo;no returns&rdquo; does not stop a not-as-described claim. The
  buyer can return the item &ldquo;even if the seller doesn&rsquo;t offer
  returns&rdquo;, and you pay for the return postage.
- You have 3 business days to respond before the buyer can ask eBay to step
  in.

Business sellers also owe buyers their legal rights. Under the Consumer
Contracts Regulations, a buyer can cancel for any reason within
[14 days of receiving the item](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/30),
and if you do not tell them about that right, the window is
[extended by up to 12 months](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/31).
eBay&rsquo;s business seller policy also asks you to accept faulty items back
within 30 days for a full refund, and to show your business contact details.

For private sellers, the main legal duty is that the item matches its
description
([Sale of Goods Act 1979, section 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/13)).
A clear description, with photos of any flaws, is your best defence against a
not-as-described claim.

## eBay and HMRC

Like Depop and Vinted, eBay has to report sellers to HMRC once they make 30
sales or receive €2,000 (about £1,700) in a calendar year
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)).
For 2026, eBay puts the money limit at £1,707, after its fees. Its
[sales reporting page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/regulatory/sales-reporting/uk-digital-sales-reporting?id=5454)
asks for your National Insurance number once you reach either limit, and eBay
can hold your payouts or block selling until you provide it.

Being reported does not mean you owe tax. Each platform counts separately, so
staying under 30 sales on each one does not change what you owe: what matters
is your total trading income across all of them. Our guide to
[tax on Depop and Vinted sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) covers
the £1,000 trading allowance and the Self Assessment dates, and the same rules
apply to eBay.

## Questions

### Is it free to sell on eBay UK?

For private sellers based in the UK, yes, except for vehicles. You get 300
free listings a month and pay no fee when an item sells. The buyer pays a
Buyer Protection fee instead. Business sellers pay a final value fee, a
per-order fee and a regulatory operating fee on every sale.

### Do I need a business account to resell clothes on eBay?

eBay&rsquo;s policy says you must register as a business if you sell items you
bought to resell. It sets no minimum number of sales. You can do that as a
sole trader; you do not need a company. Once you upgrade, you cannot switch
back.

### Does eBay charge fees on postage?

For business sellers, yes: the final value fee and the regulatory operating
fee are both worked out on the total, postage included. Private sellers pay no
selling fee, and the buyer&rsquo;s Buyer Protection fee is worked out on the
item price.

### Who pays the eBay Buyer Protection fee?

The buyer, and only on items from UK private sellers. It is already included
in the price buyers see on the listing, and checkout shows it as a separate
line. On offers and auctions, the buyer&rsquo;s bid or offer includes the fee,
so you receive a little less than the buyer offered. eBay shows you the amount
without the fee, so the figure you see is what you get.
