If you only sell your own unwanted things you probably owe no tax, but buying items to resell can make you a trader.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">

- **Clearing out your own wardrobe: you are unlikely to owe tax.** Buying things to resell at a profit is probably trading.
- **The first £1,000 of trading income a tax year is tax-free,** counted before costs, not after. Go over it and you must register for Self Assessment.
- **Depop and Vinted report you to HMRC** at 30 sales or about £1,700 in a calendar year. Being reported does not mean you owe anything.
- The Budget plans to raise the allowance to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, but the Finance Bill 2026-27 has to pass first.

## Decluttering or trading?

**Whether you owe tax depends on what you sell and why,** not on whether the platform reported you. GOV.UK says you are &ldquo;unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home, like contents of a loft or garage&rdquo;, but &ldquo;if you buy or make goods to sell at a profit, you&rsquo;re likely to be trading&rdquo;
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)).

When the line is blurry, HMRC weighs its
[badges of trade](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim20205). No single badge settles it, HMRC looks at the overall picture:

- Whether you were trying to make a profit
- How many sales you make
- Whether you changed or repaired items first
- How you sold them, and how long you held them

**Signs of trading for Depop and Vinted sellers:** buying items to resell (charity shops, car boots, wholesale or other sellers), selling regularly, and pricing for profit.

## How does the £1,000 trading allowance work?

The [trading allowance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income)
lets you have up to £1,000 of trading income a tax year tax-free, without
telling HMRC (checked against GOV.UK on 6 November 2026).

**The £1,000 is gross income, not profit.** That means everything buyers paid you, before you take off stock, postage or packaging.

Say you spent £600 on stock and sold it for £1,300 in the tax year. Your profit is £700, but your gross income is £1,300, so you must register for Self Assessment. On your return you then pick one option:

- **Claim the allowance:** take £1,000 off your income and you are taxed on £300.
- **Claim your actual expenses:** take off the £600 and you are taxed on £700.

You cannot do both, so pick whichever leaves less to tax. Costs over £1,000 make expenses the bigger deduction.

Two more things to know:

- **One allowance covers all your side income.** HMRC says the £1,000 covers all trading income combined
  ([GOV.UK, July 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/say-i-do-to-getting-your-side-hustle-tax-right)). Depop, Vinted and a bit of freelance work all count towards the same £1,000.
- **Sometimes you register even under £1,000,** for example to claim a loss or pay voluntary National Insurance. GOV.UK has the full list.

## What do Depop and Vinted tell HMRC?

Since 1 January 2024, online platforms have had to report sellers to HMRC
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)).
**You are not reported if, in a calendar year, you make fewer than 30 sales and receive less than €2,000 (about £1,700).** Hit either one and you are.

A report contains:

- Your name, address, date of birth and National Insurance number
- What you earned in the calendar year, less the platform&rsquo;s fees, in quarters

Reports go to HMRC by 31 January of the following year, and the platform has to give you a copy. This is why Depop and Vinted may ask for your National Insurance number.

- **Being reported is not a tax bill.** GOV.UK says it &ldquo;does not automatically mean you owe tax&rdquo;. Someone who sold 40 old jumpers will be reported but probably owes nothing.
- **The dates do not match.** Platforms report the calendar year (January to December). Tax runs 6 April to 5 April, so work your income out for the tax year rather than copying the platform&rsquo;s figure.

## When do I need to file?

If your trading income goes over £1,000 in a tax year,
[register for Self Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment)
by 5 October after the tax year ends. For 2025 to 2026 (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026):

- **Register** by 5 October 2026.
- **File your return online** by 31 January 2027.
- **Pay what you owe** by 31 January 2027.

Once your bill is £1,000 or more, HMRC may also ask for
[payments on account](https://www.gov.uk/understand-self-assessment-bill/payments-on-account)
towards next year&rsquo;s bill, due 31 January and 31 July.

## What is Making Tax Digital?

[Making Tax Digital for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
means digital records and quarterly updates to HMRC. **It depends on your qualifying income:** total turnover from self-employment and property, before expenses.

- **Over £50,000** in 2024 to 2025: you should have started from 6 April 2026.
- **Over £30,000** in 2025 to 2026: from 6 April 2027.
- **Over £20,000** in 2026 to 2027: from 6 April 2028.

Most side sellers are nowhere near these figures. If reselling is your full-time job, check your turnover, which is counted before costs.

## Is there tax on valuable items?

**Sell a personal possession (not trading stock) for more than £6,000 and
[Capital Gains Tax](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax-personal-possessions)
can apply to the gain.** A set sold together counts as one item, and possessions with a lifespan of less than 50 years are exempt. HMRC does not name clothing, but its examples of longer-lasting things are jewellery, works of art and antiques, so watch those, not a designer coat.

## Do I need to register for VAT?

**Only if your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months**
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat)). For second-hand goods, a
[VAT margin scheme](https://www.gov.uk/vat-margin-schemes) lets you pay VAT on the difference between what you paid and what you sold for, not the full price.

## What records should I keep?

**Keep a simple record even if you never pass £1,000.** Over it, HMRC expects
[records of all sales and expenses](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records/what-records-to-keep),
kept at least five years after that year&rsquo;s 31 January deadline. For a reseller:

- Every sale, with date and amount (your Depop and Vinted sales history helps)
- Receipts or notes for stock, including car boot and charity shop buys
- Postage, packaging and any other costs you paid
- The yearly report the platform sends you

## What about the £3,000 limit?

At Budget 2026 on 28 October, the government announced the trading allowance will rise from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, along with the Self Assessment reporting threshold
([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/income-tax-increasing-the-trading-allowance/increasing-the-trading-allowance)).

**It still has to pass into law, and it is still counted before costs.** Until then, including the tax year running to 5 April 2027, £1,000 is the figure that counts. Our [guide to the £3,000 allowance](/blog/side-hustle-tax-3000-trading-allowance) covers what changes.

## Questions

### Do I pay tax if I sell my own clothes on Vinted?

Usually not. Selling your own unwanted things is not trading, and GOV.UK says
you are unlikely to pay tax on it. The exception is a single item or set sold
for more than £6,000, where Capital Gains Tax may apply.

### Will Depop or Vinted tell HMRC about me?

Only if you make 30 or more sales, or receive €2,000 (about £1,700) or more,
in a calendar year. If you do, they send HMRC your details and earnings, and
send you a copy.

### Is the £1,000 allowance based on profit?

No. It is based on gross income, before any costs. If buyers paid you more than
£1,000 in a tax year from trading, you need to register, even if your profit
was smaller.

### I was reported to HMRC. What should I do?

Check whether you were trading. If you were only selling your own things,
there is usually nothing to do. If you were trading and your income for the
tax year was over £1,000, register for Self Assessment and file a return. If
you have missed earlier years, HMRC has guidance on how to put that right.
