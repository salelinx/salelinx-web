If a Depop buyer says an item is not as described, they have 30 days from the
purchase to raise it, you get 48 hours to reply with a solution, and if you
cannot agree Depop decides, and its decision is final. Whether you must accept a
return depends on what the buyer is claiming and whether you sell as a private
seller or a business. This guide covers the Depop process step by step, the
evidence that helps you, and how to stop claims before they start. Every rule
links Depop's or GOV.UK's own page and was checked on 6 November 2026.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">
A buyer who paid through Depop Payments can report a problem within 30 days. You
have 48 hours to answer in the app, and if you do not answer, the claim goes to
Depop automatically. Depop only steps in for "significantly" not as described:
wrong size, wrong colour, counterfeit, severe damage or major flaws you did not
list. It does not cover a buyer who changed their mind. Change of mind is your
choice if you are a private seller, but a business seller has legal duties to
take returns. Photos and honest condition notes are your best protection.
</Callout>

## Depop item not as described: what counts as a claim

Depop's page on
[what it considers "significantly not as described"](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360038455993-What-does-Depop-consider-significantly-not-as-described)
says it means the item received is "significantly different compared to the
photos or description used in the original listing". It lists what is covered:

- Incorrect sizes and wrong colours
- Counterfeit items
- Severe damage
- Major wear or flaws not detailed on the listing
- Incorrect items, or missing items in a bundle

And what is not covered:

- Smells
- Minor damage on second hand or vintage items
- Material that looks different from the images
- An item that "doesn't fit or doesn't look right"
- Items bought outside the Depop Buy button

The same page says it is the seller's responsibility to make sure the
description and photos "accurately reflect the item". Note the line about
flaws: major flaws you did not detail count against you, so what you wrote is
the whole case.

## The buyer window and how the claim runs

Depop's [My item is not as described](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791507-My-item-is-not-as-described)
page sets the steps. The buyer must raise the issue "within 30 days of the
transaction date". [Depop Protection for buyers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360038461713-Depop-Protection-for-buyers)
says the same 30 days from the date of purchase for anyone who paid through
Depop Payments (card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or Klarna).

1. **The buyer raises an issue in the app**, picks the outcome they want, writes
   a description and uploads photos. Depop says that without enough evidence it
   may not be able to proceed.
2. **You have 48 hours** to respond with a solution that takes their preferred
   outcome into account. If you do not respond at all, the dispute is
   automatically escalated to Depop.
3. **If you cannot agree,** the buyer can escalate and Depop reviews the proof
   against your original listing. It may contact you for more.
4. **Depop decides.** Its help page says "Depop's decision on item disputes is
   final", and that in some cases it may give a full or partial refund without
   the buyer returning the item.

Bundles work differently: the buyer cannot raise the issue with you directly,
and goes to Depop's support team instead.

Time matters on your side too. Check notifications, and answer inside the app,
not only by message.

## Depop Payments and PayPal

Depop Payments is the default. If the buyer paid by PayPal, Depop says the buyer
has to open a "Not as described" dispute in PayPal's Resolution Centre, and the
seller resolves it there. Depop's advice to sellers on that route is to settle
with the buyer before a dispute is raised, find your photos of the item's
condition, watch your emails and keep PayPal updated with proof. Payments made
outside the Buy button, such as a bank transfer or in-person meet-up, are not
covered by Depop Protection.

## Returns and refunds in practice

If a return is agreed, the
[refund page](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001772468-How-do-I-get-a-refund)
and the not as described page set these rules:

- The buyer must send the item back within 7 days of the return being agreed,
  with a tracked service. Depop says it will not extend this.
- Depop reimburses return postage up to £20 for UK buyers who keep their
  receipt.
- The refund is not automatic. The buyer confirms the return arrived, you
  confirm it arrived safely, and then Depop processes the refund.
- You can also refund the buyer yourself at any time. In the app, go to the
  sold item and choose Refund. A partial refund is possible too.
- If you refund a buyer who paid with Depop Payments, the fees you paid are
  refunded automatically.

Depop's guidance to sellers is to issue a full refund if the item is returned
to you.

[Depop Protection for sellers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001845367-Depop-Protection-for-sellers)
is a different thing. It covers items lost or damaged in transit, for sales up
to £250 shipped with a Depop Shipping label within 5 days. It does not cover a
buyer's not as described claim.

## What evidence helps a seller

Depop says that if a buyer claims an item is not as described, you should check
your in-app notifications and provide proof of the item's condition or
authenticity if asked. Depop reviews proof against your original listing, so the
proof that matters is what you showed and wrote when you listed it.

General guidance on what to keep:

- **Your listing photos and text.** Do not edit them after a sale. Take a
  screenshot of the sold listing.
- **Photos taken before posting.** Date-stamped photos of the item and any flaw,
  and of it packed.
- **Tracking and the label.** It proves what was sent and when.
- **The message thread.** Keep it inside Depop, so it can be seen.
- **For authenticity claims,** receipts, tags, serial numbers or authentication
  records, if you have them.

Be polite and quick. A reply that offers a fix, such as a partial refund for a
flaw you missed, often ends a claim faster than a debate.

## When you must accept returns: private and business sellers

Depop's refund page says that if a buyer has simply changed their mind,
"sellers don't have to offer you a refund": a registered business has its own
returns policy, and for everyone else "it'll be the seller's choice whether to
accept a return". A change of mind is not a not as described claim.

If you sell as a business, the law adds duties. GOV.UK's guidance on
[accepting returns and giving refunds](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds)
says: "You must offer a full refund if an item is faulty, not as described or
does not do what it's supposed to." For online sales, customers can cancel
"within 14 days of receiving their item", have another 14 days to return it, and
you must refund within 14 days of receiving the item back. Its page on
[online and distance selling](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses)
says you must tell customers they can cancel up to 14 days after delivery.

For a private seller, the main legal duty is that the item matches its
description
([Sale of Goods Act 1979, section 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/54/section/13)).
Depop's own pages add that its protection does not limit a buyer's rights
against a seller under local consumer law. Whether you count as a business
depends on how you trade. GOV.UK says that if you buy or make goods to sell at a
profit you are likely to be trading
([selling on a digital platform](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)).
If that sounds like you, read it, and check with Citizens Advice or an adviser.
This is general information, not legal advice. Our guide to
[tax on Depop and Vinted sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) covers the
trading side.

## How to prevent claims

Most not as described claims are a gap between the listing and the item.

- **Photograph every flaw close up,** and show the label, size tag and the back.
  Natural light, plain background.
- **Write the flaw in the text,** not only the photo. "Small hole near left
  hem, see photo 5" beats "good condition".
- **Give measurements.** Depop's list says incorrect sizes are covered, and
  "doesn't fit" is not, so measurements put a fit complaint on the right side.
  Say whether the size is the tag size or your measurement.
- **Describe colour honestly.** Screens vary, and "material looks different"
  is not covered, but a wrong colour is.
- **Disclose smells and wear.** Pet hair, smoke, fading, bobbling.
- **For branded or designer pieces,** keep proof of where you got them.
- **Pack and ship tracked,** and photograph the item packed.

## Questions

### How long does a buyer have to claim not as described on Depop?

30 days from the date of purchase, per Depop's help pages. The buyer must have
paid through Depop Payments to get Depop Protection. If they paid by PayPal,
they have to raise a dispute in PayPal instead.

### How long do I have to respond to a Depop claim?

48 hours from when the buyer raises the issue. If you do not respond, the
dispute is escalated to Depop automatically. If you respond and you disagree,
the buyer can escalate it to Depop for review.

### Do I have to accept a return on Depop?

If the buyer just changed their mind, a private seller can choose, and a
registered business follows its own returns policy. For a business seller, UK
law says you must offer a full refund if an item is faulty or not as described.
If Depop finds a claim valid, it can arrange a return or refund.

### Does Depop Protection cover sellers?

Only for items lost or damaged in transit, sent with a Depop Shipping label
within 5 days, on sales up to £250. It does not cover a buyer's not as described
claim. Depop has the final say on those.

### What evidence should I send Depop?

Your original listing photos and text, photos of the item before posting,
tracking, and the message thread. For authenticity claims, receipts or tags.
Depop compares the evidence with the listing, so what you wrote at the time is
what counts.

### Do I get my fees back if I refund a buyer?

Yes, for sales paid through Depop Payments. Depop says all fees are reversed
when a sale is refunded, automatically for those payments. PayPal sales need a
form submitted to Depop support.
