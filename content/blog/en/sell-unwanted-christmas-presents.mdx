## Should I return or exchange it instead of selling it?

**Yes, if you can, because you will usually get more back.** A return or exchange gives you the full price or a credit. A resale gives you a discount on the shop price, and you do the work.

Start with what the shop allows. Citizens Advice says &ldquo;lots of shops will say you can return items within 14 or sometimes even 30 days&rdquo;, but &ldquo;It&rsquo;s up to them what they offer you.&rdquo; Money Saving Expert&rsquo;s [unwanted gifts guide](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/shopping/unwanted-gifts/) adds that &ldquo;You may have longer for items bought over the &lsquo;festive period&rsquo;&rdquo;, with many retailers extending their deadlines, and tells you to check with the store.

So ask the giver for a gift receipt, and look up that retailer&rsquo;s deadline. If the deadline has gone, or it is a shop that will only give a credit note you will never use, selling is the next step.

## Do I have any legal right to return a gift?

**Not a strong one, and it belongs to the person who bought it.** There are two cases.

- **Bought online, by phone or by mail order:** Citizens Advice says &ldquo;You automatically get a 14-day &lsquo;cooling-off period&rsquo; when you buy something you haven&rsquo;t seen in person&rdquo;, and it starts &ldquo;the day after you receive your order&rdquo;. That is the buyer&rsquo;s right, and the clock may be running before you have unwrapped the present. Ask the giver early. Its [cooling-off page](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/different-ways-of-buying/buying-by-internet-mail-order-or-phone/cancelling-a-distance-sale-order/cooling-off-period-when-you-buy-goods-distance-sale/) also says that if you have a pre-paid returns label you do not pay postage, and otherwise you might.
- **Bought in a shop:** Citizens Advice says &ldquo;You don&rsquo;t have an automatic right to get your money back&rdquo; if you change your mind and there is nothing wrong with the item ([changing your mind](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/changing-your-mind-about-something-youve-bought/)). Any return is the shop&rsquo;s policy.

On gifts specifically, Money Saving Expert says &ldquo;Technically only the buyer has rights&rdquo;, that gift receipts &ldquo;aren&rsquo;t specifically covered by consumer rights law&rdquo;, and that many stores are accommodating anyway. That is a respected consumer site, not an official source. A faulty gift is a separate matter from changing your mind, and Money Saving Expert says you need a gift receipt or other proof of purchase.

## Can I list a present as new with tags?

**Only if it is genuinely unworn or unused, and the condition label differs by marketplace.**

- **eBay clothing:** &ldquo;New with tags&rdquo; means &ldquo;brand new and has never been worn. It still has the original tags and/or original packaging&rdquo;. eBay says clothing marked &ldquo;New without tags&rdquo; must have no flaws or imperfections.
- **Vinted:** its condition guide has six grades and no &ldquo;with tags&rdquo; label. &ldquo;New&rdquo; is a &ldquo;brand-new, unused, and unopened item&rdquo;, and &ldquo;Like new&rdquo; is unused but may be opened or missing packaging. If you are unsure, Vinted says to choose the lower one, and an item &ldquo;significantly worse than described&rdquo; can be cancelled or returned.
- **New underwear on Vinted:** you &ldquo;must clearly show the attached tags and/or original packaging&rdquo; in the photos.

An item you tried on once is not new with tags. Describe it honestly and photograph any marks.

## How much should I sell it for?

**Below the shop price, and lower the older and less boxed it is.** Vinted&rsquo;s [pricing help](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/376) suggests starting points as a share of retail:

- **New or Like new:** 60% to 80%.
- **Very good:** 40% to 60%.
- **Good or Satisfactory:** 20% to 40%.

It also says &ldquo;Setting a price that&rsquo;s lower than our catalogue average price might help you sell faster&rdquo;, and that a discount may help sell out-of-season items, which is worth knowing for Christmas jumpers and gift sets. Search the same item on the platform to see live prices, and see our guide to [pricing second-hand clothes](/blog/how-to-price-second-hand-clothes) for the maths. On Vinted the buyer pays the fee and on eBay private sellers pay no selling fee, so the price you set is what you receive. Our [Vinted fees](/blog/vinted-fees-uk) and [eBay fees](/blog/ebay-fees-uk) guides have the detail.

## How do I avoid scams?

**Keep the payment and the conversation on the platform.**

- **Vinted:** its [phishing help page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/628-recognise-spoof-and-phishing-messages) says to &ldquo;Avoid leaving Vinted to contact others or send payments&rdquo;, that genuine emails come from an address ending @vinted.co.uk, and to watch for messages that &ldquo;pressure you to act quickly&rdquo;. Forward suspicious emails to phishing@vinted.com.
- **eBay:** its [Safety Centre](https://pages.ebay.co.uk/safetycentre/selling.html) says &ldquo;Don&rsquo;t get caught by fake payment emails and always confirm you&rsquo;ve received a PayPal payment before sending an item&rdquo;, and to use a delivery service with online proof of delivery.
- **Depop:** its help centre says payments made outside the app are not covered by Depop Protection. We could not open that page directly, so check it at [How to buy and sell safely](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019750898-How-to-buy-and-sell-safely-on-Depop).

Posting an item because an email says you were paid is the classic mistake. Check the money in your marketplace balance or account first.

## Do I pay tax on selling unwanted presents?

**Probably not, because selling your own unwanted things is not trading.** GOV.UK says &ldquo;You&rsquo;re unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home&rdquo;, and &ldquo;If you buy or make goods to sell at a profit, you&rsquo;re likely to be trading&rdquo; ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)). A present you were given and sell on is not goods you bought to resell.

Platforms report sellers to HMRC if they make 30 or more sales or receive €2,000 or more (about £1,700) in a calendar year, as the same GOV.UK page explains. Being reported does not mean you owe tax. Our guides on [tax on Vinted and Depop sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) and [whether Vinted reports to HMRC](/blog/does-vinted-report-to-hmrc) cover it, including what changes if you start buying to sell.

## Questions

### Can I return a Christmas present without a receipt?

It depends on the shop. A gift receipt makes an exchange easier, but gift receipts are not covered by consumer rights law, and change of mind is the shop&rsquo;s policy for in-store purchases. Ask the giver for proof of purchase, or check the shop&rsquo;s policy, before you list it for sale.

### Is it legal to sell a gift I was given?

Nothing we found stops you selling a present you were given, but each marketplace bans some items, such as gift cards, opened perfume and alcohol. GOV.UK says you are unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home.

### Can I sell a gift card on Vinted, Depop or eBay?

Not easily. Vinted lists gift cards among items you cannot sell, and Depop&rsquo;s restricted content policy lists them too. eBay allows only physical paper vouchers within limits, and its voucher policy does not mention gift cards, so check eBay before listing one.

### Can I sell opened perfume or make-up I did not like?

No on Vinted and eBay. Vinted prohibits cosmetics that have been used or tried, even once, and eBay does not allow used perfume, including partly filled bottles, or items outside their original packaging. Depop lists used perfume and cosmetics as prohibited too. Sealed products in original packaging are the safer route.

### Do I need to tell HMRC if I sell my unwanted presents?

Usually not. GOV.UK says you are unlikely to pay tax on personal items, and trading means buying or making goods to sell at a profit. Platforms report sellers who make 30 sales or receive about £1,700 in a year, which is information for HMRC and not a tax bill.

### Where do I sell an unwanted gift for the most money?

It depends on the item. Clothes and shoes suit Vinted or Depop, and tech, toys and homeware suit eBay. Check what similar items are listed for on the platform, and price new items at about 60% to 80% of retail as Vinted suggests.
