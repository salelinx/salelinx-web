Vinted is the better choice for most everyday clothes, because the buyer
pays every fee and the postage, and the audience is bigger and buys faster.
Depop is the better choice for vintage, Y2K, streetwear and anything a
younger buyer would describe as a look rather than a garment, because that is
who browses there and they pay more for it. Plenty of sellers use both, and
for a wardrobe with a mix of stock that is usually the right answer.

This guide puts the two side by side on the things that change what you
earn: fees, postage, payouts, returns and buyers. Every figure comes from
Depop&rsquo;s or Vinted&rsquo;s own help pages, checked on 30 October 2026,
and each one is linked so you can check it again before you rely on it.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">
List high street, kidswear and anything under about £15 on Vinted. List
vintage, streetwear, designer and anything that sells on styling on Depop.
If you are not sure, Vinted first: it costs you nothing to find out, and you
can move an item to Depop later.
</Callout>

## Fees: what each one costs you

Neither marketplace charges a UK seller a selling fee any more. The
difference is in what happens around the sale.

**Vinted** charges sellers nothing. Its help page on the
[Vinted fee](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1653) says &ldquo;There are no
selling fees on Vinted.&rdquo; The buyer pays a Vinted fee at checkout
(renamed from the Buyer Protection fee in October 2026, same fee, same sums),
which Vinted&rsquo;s [price list](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pricelist) puts at
&ldquo;usually 3% to 8% + £0.3 to £0.8&rdquo; of the item or bundle price.
The price you set is the amount that lands in your Vinted balance.

**Depop** removed its 10% selling fee for UK listings in March 2024, but
sellers still pay payment processing. Depop&rsquo;s
[seller fees and charges](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791127-Seller-fees-and-charges)
page puts the UK rate at 2.9% plus £0.30, charged on the item price plus
postage and any tax. Buyers pay a separate
[Marketplace fee](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/21752555753361-What-is-the-Marketplace-fee)
of up to 5% of the item price plus up to £1. If you pay to boost a listing,
Depop takes 12% of the sale price on top, for listings created from 23 March
2026.

## Postage: who pays and how it works

**On Vinted the buyer picks the carrier and pays for the label at
checkout.** Vinted&rsquo;s page on
[shipping methods](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/234) lists Evri, InPost,
DPD, Royal Mail, Relay, AnyVan and DHL Express as the UK options, and says
Vinted sends the seller a digital label or code once the buyer has paid. You
can switch off some carriers in your settings, but some are on by default and
cannot be turned off. You never set a postage price, you never pay for the
label, and you cannot offer free postage.

**On Depop you choose between Depop Shipping and sorting it yourself.**
Depop Shipping gives you an Evri label (a QR code to show at a drop-off
point), the parcel is tracked, and sales up to £250 are covered by
[Depop Protection for sellers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001845367-Depop-Protection-for-sellers)
if the parcel is lost or damaged on the way. Use any other label and that
cover does not apply. If you ship yourself, you set the postage price, choose
the courier, and can offer free postage to make a listing stand out. The
label prices are shown in the app when you list.

The practical difference: Vinted postage is zero admin and zero cost, but
you have no say in how the buyer wants it sent. Depop takes a few more taps
and the processing fee is charged on the postage too, but you control the
offer.

## Payouts: when you get the money

Vinted releases your money when the buyer presses &ldquo;Everything is
OK&rdquo;, or 2 days after Vinted tells them the parcel should have arrived,
whichever is first. That is from Vinted&rsquo;s
[refund policy](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/465) and its page on
[reporting a problem](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/662). The money sits in
your Vinted balance until you withdraw it, and a withdrawal
[can take up to 5 business days](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/73) to reach
your bank.

Depop pays into your Depop balance and then transfers it to your bank
automatically. Its page on
[getting paid in the UK](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360001791147-How-do-I-get-paid-UK)
says that with Depop Shipping the payment is sent 2 business days after the
item shows as delivered (or 10 business days after the sale, whichever comes
first), and with your own postage it is 10 business days after delivery.
Funds can then take up to 5 days to appear in your bank. So a Depop sale
posted with your own label is slower to pay than the same sale on Vinted.

## Returns and protection

**Vinted:** a buyer has 2 days from the delivery notification to report an
item that never arrived, arrived damaged or is significantly not as
described. If they do not, the order completes and you are paid. Change of
mind is different: Vinted&rsquo;s page on
[returning an item](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/272) says &ldquo;it&rsquo;s
the seller&rsquo;s choice whether to accept the return&rdquo;, and the buyer
pays return postage. That is one of the main reasons Vinted suits a casual
seller: a buyer who simply changes their mind cannot force a return on you.

**Depop:** a buyer who paid through Depop Payments can claim under
[Depop Protection for buyers](https://depophelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360038461713-Depop-Protection-for-buyers)
if the item does not arrive, arrives damaged or is significantly not as
described, as long as they report it within 30 days of purchase. That is a
longer window than Vinted&rsquo;s, and Depop decides the claim. Depop does
not require sellers to accept change-of-mind returns either.

**Vinted Pro changes this.** If you register as a business on Vinted
(sole traders qualify), buyers get a legal 14-day right to return for any
reason, and Vinted holds your money until that window closes. Its
[Pro page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/pro) says Pro is free to use, and its
[Pro help page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/918) sets out the 14-day
rule. If you buy stock to resell rather than clearing your own wardrobe, you
are trading, and that applies on either marketplace. Our guide to
[tax on Vinted and Depop sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) covers
where the line is.

## Who buys on each

This is where the real difference lives, and it is the one thing you cannot
read off a help page.

**Depop** is a feed, not a catalogue. Buyers follow shops, scroll a home
feed, and search in style words: Y2K, grunge, coquette, gorpcore,
archive. Prices are higher because buyers are paying for the find and the
styling as much as the garment. A good Depop shop has a consistent look,
modelled photos, and descriptions written in the buyer&rsquo;s language. Items
that do well: vintage, band tees, streetwear and skate brands, designer and
diffusion lines, reworked and handmade pieces, and anything with a story.

**Vinted** is a search engine with filters. Buyers type a brand and a size,
filter by price and condition, and buy. They are shopping for the item, not
the shop, and they expect it to be cheap. The audience is broad: parents
buying school uniform and kidswear, people replacing a work shirt, and
plenty of buyers who have never heard of Depop. Items that do well: high
street brands in good condition, kidswear in bundles, outdoor and sports
brands, homeware, and anything priced to go.

The same Zara dress might fetch £8 on Vinted in a week and nothing on
Depop. The same 1990s band tee might sit on Vinted at £15 and sell on Depop
at £45 to someone who searched for exactly that tour.

## Which one for your wardrobe

- **Clearing out everyday clothes:** Vinted. The buyer pays the fee and the
  postage, you keep the full price, the 2-day problem window is short, and
  the audience is looking for exactly this. Price to sell and bundle the
  cheap stuff.
- **Selling vintage or a curated style:** Depop. Build a shop with a
  consistent look, model what you can, and write descriptions in the words a
  Depop buyer searches for. Accept that the processing fee and a slightly
  higher checkout total are the price of reaching those buyers.
- **Kidswear:** Vinted, in bundles by age and season. Depop has very little
  kids market.
- **Designer and higher-value pieces:** Depop first, for the buyer who will
  pay the price. Vinted&rsquo;s buyers are price-led, so a £120 coat tends to
  attract low offers there. If it does not move on Depop in a month, list it
  on Vinted at a realistic price.
- **Branded mid-range (Nike, Carhartt, Levi&rsquo;s, The North Face):** both.
  These have buyers on each marketplace and the right price differs, so
  checking sold prices on both before you list is worth the five minutes.

Timing matters a bit on either marketplace. Evenings and Sunday nights are
the usual advice, and our guide to the
[best time to post on Vinted](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-vinted) goes
through what Vinted has actually said about it.

## Selling on both

If you sell on both, the work is in not typing every listing twice and not
selling the same item twice. Two rules whichever tool you use. Read
Vinted&rsquo;s [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions) first:
they restrict external software tools and bulk deleting and re-listing, so
keep anything you do on Vinted to the pace and volume of a person listing by
hand. And delete the other copy the moment an item sells. A buyer paying for
an item you have already posted to someone else is the fastest way to a bad
review on both.

## Questions

### Is Depop or Vinted better for a beginner?

Vinted. There is nothing to set up beyond a bank account, the buyer pays
every fee and the postage, you never price a label, and the problem window
after delivery is 2 days. Depop rewards a shop with a look, which takes
longer to build.

### Which has lower fees, Depop or Vinted?

Vinted. Sellers pay nothing. Depop sellers pay 2.9% plus £0.30 in payment
processing on the item and postage. Buyers pay a fee on both: up to 5% plus
up to £1 on Depop, and usually 3% to 8% plus £0.30 to £0.80 on Vinted.

### Can I sell the same item on Depop and Vinted at the same time?

Yes, there is no rule against it on either marketplace. The risk is selling
it twice. Take the other listing down as soon as one sells, or use a
crosslisting app that links the two copies and flags the sale.

### Do I pay tax on Depop or Vinted sales?

Selling your own old clothes is not taxable. Buying stock to resell is
trading, and HMRC&rsquo;s
[trading allowance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income)
is £1,000 a year before you need to report it. Both marketplaces report
seller data to HMRC above certain thresholds. Our guide to
[tax on Vinted and Depop sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) has
the figures and the gov.uk links.

### Does Vinted have more buyers than Depop in the UK?

Vinted is the larger marketplace in the UK by most measures, and its buyers
are broader: it covers kidswear, homeware and everyday high street brands
that Depop barely touches. Depop&rsquo;s audience is smaller and younger but
spends more per item on the styles it wants. Neither publishes current UK
buyer numbers, so treat any exact figure you see as an estimate.
