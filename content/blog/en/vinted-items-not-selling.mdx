When Vinted items are not selling, the cause is almost always price, the first photo or a vague title, and you fix them in that order before you pay for anything. Extra promotion on a badly priced item with a weak photo just shows the problem to more people. This guide is general guidance built on what Vinted&rsquo;s own help pages say, checked on 6 November 2026. It is not our own sales data, and nothing here guarantees a sale.

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">
Work down this list and stop when the item sells. One: compare your price with similar listings, buyer total included. Two: make the first photo show the whole item. Three: rewrite the title with brand, colour and type. Four: fill every field. Five: check postage and the buyer&rsquo;s total. Six: send offers to people who favourited. Seven: relist or Bump only an item that has already passed the first six. Timing is a tiebreaker, not a fix.
</Callout>

## Why your Vinted items are not selling: start with the listing

Most unsold items fail at one of two moments. Either nobody clicks, which points at the photo, title and price in search, or people click and leave, which points at the price, condition detail or the total at checkout. Vinted&rsquo;s page on [recommended content](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/409) says search ranking weighs item attractiveness, including price, condition and how many interactions a listing gets. So a listing nobody opens tends to fall further down. That is why the order below starts with the things a buyer sees first.

If you can see views and favourites on an item, they tell you which moment is failing. Few views means people are not finding or opening it. Plenty of views and no favourites means the price or the detail is turning them away.

## 1. Price against similar listings

Vinted&rsquo;s [pricing help page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/376) suggests two things. Search the catalogue for similar items to see how other members price them. And &ldquo;Setting a price that&rsquo;s lower than our catalogue average price might help you sell faster.&rdquo; It also gives rough starting points by condition: new or like new at 60% to 80% of retail price, very good at 40% to 60%, and good or satisfactory at 20% to 40%.

Treat those as a starting range, not a rule. A rare brand can beat it and a common high street piece often sits below it. The useful check is to look at the same brand, item type, size and condition, and compare your price with the lower half of what you find. I could not confirm that Vinted shows buyers the final price of sold items, so use live listings as your guide and be wary of treating a high asking price on a listing that has sat for months as proof of value.

Also keep the season in mind. The same page says a discount may help sell items that do not fit the current season. Our [clothing pricing guide](/blog/how-to-price-second-hand-clothes) goes through the maths in more detail.

## 2. The first photo

Vinted&rsquo;s [Catalogue Rules](https://www.vinted.co.uk/catalog-rules) say &ldquo;The first photo should clearly represent the whole item.&rdquo; Photos must show the item as it is, with no image editing, and cannot be stock photos, photos from ads or watermarked images. You can upload up to 20.

The first photo is the one that appears in search and the feed, so it does the selling. A flat lay or a hanger shot in daylight, whole item in frame, plain background. Then add close-ups of labels, fabric and every flaw. Vinted&rsquo;s [describing an item](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/49) page asks you to show flaws in the photos as well as the description.

## 3. Title and search terms

The same help page says to include specific details such as brand, colour, type or unique features, and to avoid words like &ldquo;nice&rdquo;, &ldquo;pretty&rdquo; or &ldquo;good&rdquo;. Its example prefers &ldquo;New pink Zara mini evening dress&rdquo; to &ldquo;Very nice dress&rdquo;.

Buyers search in plain words: brand, item, colour, size. Put those words in the title and the description, in the order you would say them. Do not add other brand names or hashtags to look like a bigger search term. The Catalogue Rules say using other brands that are not related to your item in the brand field, title, description or as a hashtag &ldquo;is not allowed&rdquo;, and &ldquo;inspired by&rdquo; followed by a brand is generally prohibited too. For how search reads all of this, see our guide to the [Vinted algorithm and search](/blog/vinted-algorithm-and-search).

## 4. Category, size, brand and condition fields

Search filters work on the fields, not on your title. Vinted says on its [recommended content page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/409) that filters are applied first and the remaining listings are then ranked. If the category is wrong, the size is blank or the brand is missing, a buyer who filters never sees the item, however good the title is.

Check each field against the label and the photos:

- **Category.** Upload each item in its corresponding category, as the Catalogue Rules require.
- **Size.** Use the size on the label, and say in the description if it runs small or large.
- **Brand.** Pick the brand in the brand field. Do not add other brands in the description.
- **Condition.** Choose it honestly. Buyers can return an item that is significantly different from the listing.
- **Colour and materials.** Fill them in, and note extra materials in the description.

## 5. Postage and the buyer&rsquo;s total

Vinted says postage is always paid by the buyer, and buyers also pay a Vinted fee on top of your price. Our [Vinted fees guide](/blog/vinted-fees-uk) has the figures and sources. What matters here is that the buyer compares your price plus the fee plus postage, not your price alone. A £4 item with £3 postage and a fee can look poor value next to a £6 item in a bundle.

Vinted also says that offering different shipping options may attract more buyers, as they have more delivery choices. See its [selling faster page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/57). Offering bundle discounts is on the same page. A buyer taking several items pays one fee on the bundle, which makes cheap stock easier to move.

## 6. Favourites and sending offers

A favourite is a buyer saying they are interested. Vinted&rsquo;s [selling faster page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/57) says: &ldquo;If a buyer favourites your item or asks about it, you can send them an offer. This may help them decide to buy.&rdquo;

On its [offers page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/258), Vinted says sellers can offer up to 40% less than the original cost, that there is no limit on the number of seller offers, and that the new price is only visible to the person you send it to. Other buyers still see the original price. Our guide to [sending offers on Vinted](/blog/how-to-send-offers-ebay-vinted-depop) shows the steps.

Start with a modest cut, not the full 40%. The goal is to give a keen buyer a reason to act, not to give the item away.

## 7. Relisting stale items

If an item has had a fair run with a good photo, title and price and still sits, a fresh listing can help. Vinted has no relist button, so this means uploading again and deleting the old one. Vinted&rsquo;s [terms](https://www.vinted.co.uk/terms-and-conditions) forbid using external software tools such as bots, and forbid deleting and re-listing &ldquo;the same Item multiple times or multiple Items in bulk&rdquo;. The [help page on hidden and removed listings](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/62) says &ldquo;The same items can&rsquo;t be listed twice on Vinted&rdquo;, so never leave the old and new listing live together.

So relist by hand, once per item, and only after fixing the real cause. Changing nothing and relisting just repeats the same listing. Our [relisting guide](/blog/how-to-relist-on-vinted) covers the safe way.

## 8. Bumps and Showcase

Vinted says an item Bump is a paid feature you can add to any listing &ldquo;to increase your chance of selling the item&rdquo;, and that it makes the item appear higher in member newsfeeds and search results for 3 or 7 consecutive days, or until it sells. The price is shown at payment. See its [Bump page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/340). Showcase highlights up to 15 listings for 7 days ([Showcase page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/452)).

A Bump puts the item in front of more eyes. It does not fix a price that is too high or a photo that is unclear. Use it last, on one item that has passed steps 1 to 6. Our [Bump cost guide](/blog/vinted-bump-cost-and-does-it-work) goes through when it is worth paying for.

## 9. Timing

Timing sits at the bottom because it matters least. Vinted&rsquo;s help pages do not give an hour-by-hour guide to listing times. The usual advice is to list in the evening, and our [best time to post on Vinted](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-vinted) guide separates what Vinted has said from received wisdom. Use it as a tiebreaker when you are posting an item that is already priced and photographed well.

## Questions

### Why are my Vinted items not selling?

Usually price, the first photo or a vague title. Compare your price with similar listings including the buyer&rsquo;s total, make the first photo show the whole item, and put brand, colour and type in the title. Fill every field too, because buyers filter by them. Paid promotion comes last.

### Should I lower my price on Vinted?

If similar items are listed for less and yours has had views but no favourites, yes. Vinted says a price below its catalogue average might help you sell faster. Lower it in steps, or send an offer to people who favourited, rather than one large cut.

### Does Vinted hide items that do not sell?

Vinted does not say that it hides them. Its help pages say search ranking considers item attractiveness, such as how recent a listing is, its interactions, price and condition. An older listing with few interactions can sit lower, so a fix to the listing is better than waiting.

### Is it worth paying for a Bump on an item that is not selling?

Only after the price, first photo and title are right. A Bump makes an item appear higher in newsfeeds and search results for 3 or 7 days, or until it sells, according to Vinted. It does not change what the buyer sees when they open the item.

### Can I relist an item that has not sold?

You can upload it again by hand and delete the old one, but not keep both live. Vinted does not allow the same item to be listed twice, and its terms forbid deleting and re-listing in bulk or using bots. Relist rarely, and change the cause first.

### Do offers help items sell on Vinted?

They can. Vinted suggests sending an offer to a buyer who favourites your item or asks about it. Sellers can offer up to 40% off the original price, and only the person who gets the offer sees the new price.
