At Budget 2026 on 28 October, the government said it will treble the trading
allowance, the tax-free amount for small side incomes, from £1,000 to £3,000.
It starts on 6 April 2027. For resellers on Depop, Vinted and eBay it is the
biggest change to the &ldquo;side hustle tax&rdquo; rules since platforms
started reporting sellers to HMRC in 2024. This guide covers what changes,
what does not, and why the £1,000 limit still matters for the tax year you
are in now.

<Callout title="Not tax advice">
This is general information, checked against GOV.UK on 1 November 2026. The
change still has to pass into law, and your own situation may be different.
If you are unsure, use HMRC&rsquo;s
[online checker](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-tell-hmrc-about-your-income-from-online-platforms)
or speak to an accountant.
</Callout>

<Callout variant="tip" title="The short answer">
From 6 April 2027, if your trading income for the tax year is £3,000 or less,
it is tax-free and you do not need to tell HMRC. The £3,000 counts what buyers
paid you, before your costs. Until then the limit is still £1,000, and that
includes the tax year running now, from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. If you
only sell your own unwanted things, none of this applies to you.
</Callout>

## What was announced

HMRC&rsquo;s
[policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/income-tax-increasing-the-trading-allowance/increasing-the-trading-allowance),
published on Budget day, says the measure &ldquo;increases the amount of the
trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000&rdquo;, with effect &ldquo;from
6 April 2027, for the tax year 2027 to 2028 onwards&rdquo;. The Self
Assessment reporting threshold for trading income rises to £3,000 at the same
time, so the two stay in step
([Budget 2026 overview, paragraph 1.5](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar)).

HMRC estimates that:

- up to 1.4 million people could benefit;
- about 400,000 will pay less tax, by £380 a year on average;
- about 100,000 will no longer need to report their trading income at all.

It is not law yet. The change goes into the Finance Bill 2026-27, which
Parliament still has to pass.

## How this differs from the 2025 plan

You may remember an earlier &ldquo;£3,000&rdquo; headline. In
[March 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-side-hustlers-as-300000-people-to-be-taken-out-of-tax-returns-government-announces)
the government said it would raise only the reporting threshold to £3,000,
&ldquo;within this parliament&rdquo;. The tax-free allowance would have stayed
at £1,000, so income between £1,000 and £3,000 would still have been taxed,
just reported through a simpler online service instead of a full tax return.

The Budget goes further. It raises the allowance itself, so under the new
rules that £1,000 to £3,000 band is tax-free as well as easier to report.

## It still counts sales, not profit

The part most people get wrong stays the same. The allowance is measured
against your **gross** trading income: everything buyers paid you, before you
take off what you spent on stock, postage or packaging
([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income)).
The Budget overview says the same: you no longer declare or pay tax on the
income &ldquo;where the allowances cover all their relevant income before
expenses&rdquo;.

If you go over, you still choose one of two deductions on your return: take
off the allowance, or take off your actual costs. Not both. A bigger allowance
makes the first option worth a lot more. Here are two resellers in each
system:

## What stays the same

- **Selling your own things is not trading.** GOV.UK says you are
  &ldquo;unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home&rdquo;
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform)).
  The allowance only matters if you buy or make things to sell at a profit.
  Our [guide to tax on Depop and Vinted sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales)
  explains where that line sits.
- **Platforms still report you.** Depop, Vinted and eBay send HMRC your details
  once you make 30 sales or receive €2,000 (about £1,700) in a calendar year.
  The Budget did not change that. Being reported does not mean you owe tax.
- **It is one allowance for all of it.** HMRC&rsquo;s
  [side hustle guide](https://taxhelpforhustles.campaign.gov.uk/) says
  &ldquo;you have a single £1,000 tax-free allowance for each tax year&rdquo;,
  and income from different side hustles counts towards it. From April 2027,
  Depop, Vinted, eBay and a bit of freelance work all share the one £3,000.
- **Some people register anyway.** For example, to claim a loss, to pay
  voluntary Class 2 National Insurance, or to claim Tax-Free Childcare or
  Maternity Allowance based on self-employment
  ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income)).
- **The property allowance is separate.** It covers income from renting out
  property, stays at £1,000, and was not part of this change.
- **Bigger thresholds do not move.** Making Tax Digital and VAT registration
  depend on much larger figures, covered in our
  [main tax guide](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales).

## The VAT change for online marketplaces

The Budget also plans to extend the rules that make online marketplaces
collect VAT to sales by UK businesses that are VAT registered or sell over
£90,000 a year on one platform, from October 2027. It &ldquo;will not apply to non-business
(i.e. personal) sales, second-hand goods, or business-to-business
transactions&rdquo;
([Budget 2026 overview, paragraph 1.55](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar)).
For a seller of second-hand clothes it is unlikely to change anything. The
threshold and the start date are still to be confirmed in 2027.

## What to do now

1. **Work out this tax year so far.** Add up what buyers have paid you since
   6 April 2026 across every platform. Over £1,000 and trading means
   registering by 5 October 2027.
2. **Keep records as if you will file.** Sales, stock receipts, postage and
   packaging. Near the £3,000 line, the receipts decide whether claiming costs
   or the allowance is better.
3. **Check again before April 2027.** The figures above are what the
   government has announced. If the Finance Bill changes them, we will update
   this guide.

## Questions

### Is the £3,000 trading allowance in force now?

No. It starts on 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 2028 tax year, and the Finance
Bill still has to pass. Until then the allowance is £1,000.

### Is the £3,000 before or after costs?

Before. It is measured against what buyers paid you. If your sales are over
£3,000, you register even if your profit is lower, then choose between
deducting £3,000 or your actual costs.

### Do I need to tell HMRC about what I sell before April 2027?

Possibly. If you are trading and your income between 6 April 2026 and
5 April 2027 is over £1,000, you need to register by 5 October 2027. The new
allowance does not reach back to that year.

### Is it £3,000 for each platform?

No. It is one allowance for all your trading income in the tax year, however
many platforms or side hustles it comes from.

### Will Vinted, Depop or eBay still report me to HMRC?

Yes, if you make 30 sales or receive €2,000 (about £1,700) in a calendar year.
That rule did not change. A report is information for HMRC, not a tax bill.
