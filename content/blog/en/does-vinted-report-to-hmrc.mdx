## Does Vinted report to HMRC? The rules

GOV.UK&rsquo;s page on [selling goods or services on a digital platform](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform) says &ldquo;New rules started in the UK from 1 January 2024.&rdquo; Platforms must collect information about sellers each calendar year and send it to HMRC by the following January. The rules come from the OECD&rsquo;s model for digital platform reporting, brought into UK law through the Platform Operators (Due Diligence and Reporting Requirements) Regulations 2023. Platforms report the calendar year, 1 January to 31 December, not the tax year.

Your details are not reported if you make fewer than 30 sales of goods in a calendar year and receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700) for them, GOV.UK says. Meet either one and they are.

Vinted&rsquo;s own help pages match. Its [HMRC requirements page](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1149-hlaseni-dac7) says it contacts you if you have completed 30 sales or more, or sold over €2,000 (about £1,700), within the calendar year. Vinted&rsquo;s pages word the second test slightly differently from each other and from GOV.UK (&ldquo;sold over&rdquo;, &ldquo;earns over&rdquo; and &ldquo;receive&rdquo;), so treat GOV.UK as the authority.

## What gets shared

GOV.UK says that for individuals the platform reports your full name, address, date of birth and tax identification number. It also reports your income. The amounts are &ldquo;broken down into quarters of the calendar year&rdquo; and are &ldquo;less any fees, commission or taxes deducted by the platform&rdquo;. The platform has to give you a copy of what it sent.

Vinted&rsquo;s [What is HMRC reporting](https://www.vinted.co.uk/help/1308) page says it sends the information of everyone who submitted a form to the relevant authorities, in January of the following year. It sends you a form that is pre-filled from your account, and the main thing it asks for is your National Insurance number.

Vinted says the information is encrypted and not used for anything else. The Vinted pages do not list every field they share, so GOV.UK&rsquo;s list is the one to rely on.

## When it happens

- **During the year.** Once you cross a threshold, Vinted contacts you, and tells you to complete the form by the end of that calendar year. If you hear nothing, Vinted says you do not need to do anything.
- **By 31 January.** The platform sends its report to HMRC. For example, 2026 information is due by 31 January 2027, and you receive a copy.
- **After that.** HMRC holds the information. Nothing happens automatically, and no tax is due because of the report.

## Being reported is not a tax bill

GOV.UK says a platform reporting your details to HMRC &ldquo;does not automatically mean you owe tax&rdquo;. Vinted says completing a seller report &ldquo;doesn&rsquo;t mean there&rsquo;s an obligation to pay taxes on your Vinted sales&rdquo; and that selling personal items on Vinted is generally not taxed.

HMRC receives the report to compare with what people tell it. If you were trading and told HMRC, the numbers should match. If you were not trading, there is nothing to match. The report changes no tax law.

Two limits are worth knowing:

- **The threshold is a platform rule, not a tax rule.** eBay&rsquo;s help page says the thresholds &ldquo;only relate to whether eBay is required to report your data&rdquo;. They do not decide whether you owe tax.
- **Staying under it does not switch off tax.** If you are trading and your trading income is over the allowance, you need to tell HMRC whether or not any platform reported you.

## Selling your things or trading

This is the question that decides tax, so it is worth one short section. GOV.UK says &ldquo;You&rsquo;re unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home, like contents of a loft or garage&rdquo;, and &ldquo;If you buy or make goods to sell at a profit, you&rsquo;re likely to be trading&rdquo;.

If you are clearing out your own wardrobe, reported or not, you are generally fine. If you buy stock to sell on, you are probably trading, and the trading allowance comes in. Our [guide to tax on Depop and Vinted sales](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) explains the line between the two and the key dates. The £1,000 allowance is counted before costs, and the Budget plans to raise it to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. That is not law yet, and our [trading allowance guide](/blog/side-hustle-tax-3000-trading-allowance) covers it.

The two figures are different things. 30 sales or about £1,700 decides whether a platform reports you. The allowance decides whether trading income needs telling to HMRC. A declutterer can pass the first and owe nothing. A reseller can stay under the first and still need to register.

## Depop and eBay follow the same rules

The rules apply to digital platforms in general, so Depop and eBay sit under the same regime as Vinted. eBay&rsquo;s [UK digital sales reporting page](https://www.ebay.co.uk/help/account/regulatory/sales-reporting/uk-digital-sales-reporting?id=5454) confirms it. It says it reports annually to HMRC, from January 2025, if you complete 30 or more sales or your total sales are £1,707 or more after fees and commissions. It lists the data it sends: name, address and National Insurance number, quarterly transaction counts and amounts, quarterly fees and taxes withheld, and the bank account that receives your proceeds. It adds that the rules do not create new tax obligations for you. We could not find a Depop help page to cite, so check the Depop app for its wording. The GOV.UK rules above apply to it all the same.

As we read the rules, each platform counts the sales you make on its own platform. Your tax position adds everything together, so £1,500 sold on each of three platforms might go unreported by all three and still be £4,500 of income to consider.

## What to do if you are reported

1. **Do not panic.** It is a routine report, and many sellers get one.
2. **Check what you were doing.** Selling your own things is generally not taxed. Buying to resell is trading.
3. **Add up your trading income for the tax year**, not the calendar year, across every platform, before costs.
4. **Over the allowance and trading?** Register for Self Assessment. Our [tax guide](/blog/tax-on-vinted-and-depop-sales) has the dates.
5. **Keep records** of sales, stock and postage either way.

If you are unsure, an accountant or HMRC can tell you. We cannot.

## Questions

### Does Vinted report to HMRC?

Yes, if you make 30 or more sales, or receive about £1,700 (€2,000) or more, in a calendar year. It sends your details and your sales totals to HMRC by 31 January the following year and gives you a copy.

### Does Vinted report every seller to HMRC?

No. Sellers below both thresholds are not reported. You are reported if you meet either one, so 30 sales is enough even if you earned little.

### Do I owe tax if Vinted reports me?

Not because of the report. GOV.UK says being reported does not automatically mean you owe tax. Selling your own unwanted items is generally not taxed. If you buy things to resell, the trading rules apply instead.

### What information does Vinted send HMRC?

GOV.UK says platforms report your name, address, date of birth and tax identification number, plus your income by quarter after fees. Vinted asks you for your National Insurance number on a pre-filled form.

### When does HMRC get the information?

By 31 January after the calendar year ends. Information for 2026 is due by 31 January 2027.

### Do Depop and eBay report to HMRC too?

Yes. They fall under the same rules. eBay&rsquo;s help page confirms its reporting and gives its threshold as 30 sales or £1,707 after fees.
